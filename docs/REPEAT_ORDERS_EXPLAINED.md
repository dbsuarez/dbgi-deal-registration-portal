# Repeat Orders, NOA, and NTP Explained

**As of:** 2026-10-30  
**Related decision:** D1 in [DECISION_RESPONSES_2026-10-30.md](DECISION_RESPONSES_2026-10-30.md)

A plain-language explanation of the terms behind DBGI's repeat-order rule.

## The two letters: NOA and NTP

When a government agency buys something, it sends the winning supplier two official letters.

| Letter  | Full name         | What it says                       | When it arrives                          |
| ------- | ----------------- | ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| **NOA** | Notice of Award   | "You won. We are buying from you." | After the agency chooses the winning bid |
| **NTP** | Notice to Proceed | "Go ahead and deliver."            | After the contract is signed             |

Think of the NOA as "you got the job" and the NTP as "start now".

## What a repeat order is

A **repeat order** is when the agency buys **more of the same item, from the same supplier**, based on an
earlier purchase, instead of running a new bidding. The agency follows the government procurement rules for
when a repeat order is allowed; DBGI does not need to check those limits.

## An example

1. A city hall buys **10 printers** through a DBGI partner. DBGI approved a special price for that deal.
2. A few months later, the city hall needs **5 more of the same printer**. Instead of a new bidding, it makes a
   repeat order.
3. The city hall sends the partner a new **NOA** and **NTP** for the 5 printers, and the letters say it is a
   **repeat order**.
4. The partner asks DBGI: "Can I get the same special price for these 5?"

## DBGI's rule (decision D1)

DBGI gives the same approved special price for a repeat order **only if both are true**:

1. **Same end-user.** The repeat order comes from the same agency as the original deal (in the example, the
   same city hall).
2. **Proof.** The partner provides the agency's **NOA and NTP**, and they state that it is a **repeat order**.

If either is missing (a different agency, no letters, or letters that do not say "repeat order"), DBGI
**declines** the request. There is no special price; the partner can register it as a new deal instead.

## What this means for the portal

This will be built in the repeat-order milestone, after pricing:

- A repeat-order request is linked to the original deal.
- The partner must upload the NOA and NTP.
- The portal checks that the end-user matches the original deal.
- DBGI accepts or declines the request, and the decision and reason are recorded.

This is stricter than the original plan, which said proof for repeat orders was not mandatory.

## Quick glossary

| Term          | Meaning                                                                           |
| ------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| End-user      | The government agency or organization that buys and uses the product              |
| Partner       | The dealer company that sells to the end-user and registers the deal with DBGI    |
| Special price | The discounted price DBGI approves for a registered deal                          |
| NOA           | Notice of Award: the agency's letter saying the supplier won                      |
| NTP           | Notice to Proceed: the agency's letter saying the supplier can start delivering   |
| Repeat order  | The agency buys more of the same item from the same supplier, without new bidding |
